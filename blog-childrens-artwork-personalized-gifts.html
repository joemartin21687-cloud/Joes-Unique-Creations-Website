Turning a Child’s Drawing Into a Personalized Gift

A child’s drawing can say more than a polished design ever could. The uneven letters, the unusual colors and the way they draw their family are often the parts you want to keep. If you’re thinking about turning that picture into a personalized gift, start by deciding what makes the original special to you.

At Joe’s Unique Creations in Glenside, Pennsylvania, children’s artwork can be reviewed for printing on a compatible personalized gift. Here are a few ways to prepare the picture and explain your idea so the important details stay at the center of the design conversation.

Choose the drawing before choosing the gift

Put a few possible pictures in front of you and think about the person receiving the gift. A drawing of a grandparent, a handwritten name or a picture of a family pet might have a connection they’ll recognize right away. You don’t need to choose the most detailed piece. Pick the one that feels like the child who made it.

Look at the shape of the artwork, too. A long row of people and a small centered flower may suit different layouts. Decide whether you want the whole page included or whether a particular part should be the focus. Keep the complete original available even if you’re considering a crop.

Make a clear copy of the original

A clear scan or well-lit photograph gives us something useful to review. Lay the page flat, keep the camera facing it squarely and check that your hands or phone aren’t casting a shadow across the drawing. Look over the picture before sending it to see whether all the edges and important marks are visible.

If the page has writing near the bottom, include it in your copy. A date or a small signature can be part of what makes the gift meaningful. Send the best version you have, and mention any faint areas that are clearer on the paper than they appear in the photo. My artwork file guide explains why the original image is helpful when reviewing a printing project.

Say which details should stay exactly as drawn

Tell me if you want the handwriting, spelling and colors preserved as they are. A backwards letter or a funny spelling might be the detail that makes a parent smile. Put that preference in your notes so it’s clear that the unusual part belongs in the finished design.

Also mention any changes you do want to discuss. Perhaps there is an unrelated mark in the corner, or you want to add a typed name beneath the drawing. Keep a copy of the untouched artwork and describe the proposed edits separately. That gives us a reference when reviewing the layout together.

Match the layout to where the gift will be used

Think about whether the recipient will display the gift or use it during the day. A mug, a photo tile and a keepsake for a shelf each give the drawing a different setting. Consider how much space the artwork needs and whether adding a message would leave enough room for the picture.

The personalized gift page includes options to discuss, along with information about artwork review. Ask about the particular product and printable area before settling on a layout. If you’re ordering several copies for relatives, list any names or messages that change from one gift to another.

Review the small personal touches before approval

Check the proof against the original drawing, including the signature and any wording you asked to keep. Read added names and dates carefully. If the gift is a surprise, choose one person to collect corrections so the instructions stay together. Include the date you need the gift in hand and confirm availability and timing for your order. The aim is a layout that gives the drawing room to be itself and lets the person receiving it recognize the child behind it.
