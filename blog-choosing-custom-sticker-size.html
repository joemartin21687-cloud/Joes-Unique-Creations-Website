How to Choose the Right Size for Custom Stickers and Labels

When someone asks me what size sticker to order, I like to start with where it’s going. A logo that looks right on the front of a paper bag can take up too much room on a small box. A label that fits a jar can still be hard to read if the important words curve out of sight. A ruler and a piece of paper can save a lot of guessing.

At Joe’s Unique Creations in Glenside, Pennsylvania, I print custom stickers and labels for packaging, events, businesses and personal projects. Here’s how I’d work through the size and layout before sending in an order.

Measure the spot where the sticker will go

Put the actual item in front of you and measure the area you want to cover. On a box, check where the lid folds and where the edges meet. On a bottle, look for the part that stays the same width from top to bottom. If you’re labeling a bag, measure it with something inside so you can see how the front will look when it’s filled.

Write the width and height in inches. For a round sticker, give the diameter. If you want the sticker to close a fold in a bag, mark how much of the design should be visible on the front after it folds over. Those details tell me more than the overall size of the bag or box.

Cut out a paper version before you settle on a size

Draw two possible sizes on paper, cut them out and hold them against the item. You don’t need to print the design for this first check. Look at how much space is left around the edges and whether one size feels more balanced. If the label will go on several package sizes, try both cutouts on the smallest package first.

Once you have a size you like, sketch the main words or print the artwork at that size. Read it from the distance you expect someone to use it. A label someone holds in their hand can work with a different layout from a sticker meant to catch their eye across a display table.

Make room for the information people need first

Choose one thing you want the customer to see first. On a bag seal, that might be your logo. On a product label, it may be the product name or a detail that distinguishes one version from another. Give that information enough room before deciding where to place the smaller text.

If the design feels crowded on paper, try arranging it across a wider shape or moving secondary details to a separate panel. A round sticker can be a good fit for a centered mark, while a rectangle may give a long name more room. The right choice depends on the artwork and the spot you measured.

Tell me how you’ll use it

Mention the surface and what the sticker will be exposed to. For example, a label for a container that goes in a cooler raises different questions from a sticker you put on a paper gift bag. Tell me what you need the sticker to handle so we can discuss material options for your project.

If you have several designs, list the size and quantity for each one. A note like “50 logo stickers and 50 labels” is a start, but adding the dimensions and where each goes makes it much easier to review. Use the same names on the files and in your list so it’s clear which artwork belongs to which item.
