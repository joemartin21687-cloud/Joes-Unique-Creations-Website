Where Should the Design Go on a Custom Hoodie?

If you’re ordering a custom hoodie, take a minute to picture it on somebody. Are they standing behind a table at a school fundraiser, walking away from you at a team event, or working outside with a jacket over it? The same logo can do a different job depending on where it goes. Here at Joe’s Unique Creations in Glenside, I like to figure out what people should see first before we decide where to put the artwork.

Give the front one clear job

A small left-chest design makes sense when you want a simple mark people can see up close. Think of a short shop name, a club emblem, or a pair of initials. A logo with a phone number and three lines of tiny lettering may be easier to read as a larger front design. Try looking at the logo at the size you have in mind. If you have to lean in to read it on paper, decide which words are worth keeping at that size.

With a full-front design on a pullover, look at the pocket and the drawstrings on the garment you’re considering. Hold a piece of paper where you want the design and let the strings hang naturally. If you’re considering a zip-up, think about what the design should look like when the hoodie is both open and closed. A small mark on one side can make sense when you want the artwork to stay together with the zipper open.

Check the back with the hood down

Lay a hoodie on a table with the hood resting on the back. If your first idea is to put a team name high between the shoulders, check where the bottom of the hood falls. You may want to move the name lower, or use that upper space for a detail that can be partly covered. Try the same check with the hoodie on a person. The way it hangs gives you a better idea of where the artwork will sit during regular wear.

If the back is meant to help somebody spot a crew or recognize a team, choose one piece of information to make prominent. That might be the company name or the team name. Take a few steps back from your sketch and see what you can read first. The exercise can settle a debate about layout faster than comparing two images at full size on a phone.

Decide what belongs on the sleeve

A sleeve can be a good place for a short detail, like a year or a player number. Mark the left or right sleeve from the wearer’s point of view so there’s no confusion. If you picture lettering running down the arm, note where you want it to begin and which way it should read. A quick sketch with an arrow can help when the same artwork could be turned more than one way.

Use a real hoodie to check your idea

Cut a paper rectangle about the size of each design and try the placements together on a hoodie you already own. If you’re ordering several garment sizes, look at the smallest and largest you plan to include. Think about whether the design should feel similar across the order or whether a smaller size needs its own layout. Our custom hoodies and sweatshirts page is a place to start when you’re deciding on a style, and the artwork file guide can help you choose which version of your logo to use. A picture of your paper layout can also show how you want the different pieces to fit together.
