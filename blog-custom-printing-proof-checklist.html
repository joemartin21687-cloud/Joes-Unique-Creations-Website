What to Check Before Approving Your Custom Printing Proof

When a design proof arrives, it is tempting to take a quick look, decide it looks good and move on. But those few minutes before approval are a useful chance to check the details that matter most. A beautiful layout can still contain a misspelled name, an old phone number or a placement that differs from what you pictured.

At Joe’s Unique Creations in Glenside, Pennsylvania, I want customers to feel comfortable discussing their artwork. You do not need design experience to review a proof carefully. Start with the message, then work through the layout one part at a time.

Read the words without relying on memory

Look at every line as if you are seeing it for the first time. Check names, business names, dates and event titles against the information you intended to provide. Familiar words are easy to skim past, especially when you have been discussing the same design for several days.

For contact information, compare each digit of a phone number and each character of a website address. If you supplied an older logo, make sure it does not contain information you meant to replace. Copying an existing file does not automatically make its wording current.

Check which version you are reviewing

Keep track of the latest proof and the corrections you requested. If several versions have been shared, identify the one you are approving clearly. A short reply referring to the latest image or version is more useful than an approval attached to an earlier conversation.

For group orders, have one organizer collect feedback before sending it back. That gives everyone a chance to check their part while keeping the instructions together. If two people ask for different changes to the same detail, settle that choice before treating the layout as final.

Look at the background and the edges

Decide whether the design should include a rectangular background or whether only the artwork itself should appear. My artwork file guide explains why an unwanted background can become part of a printed design. Tell me if you expected the space around a logo or photograph to be removed.

Also check the crop around important subjects. Are the faces, names or parts of the logo that matter to you still visible? If a photograph has been trimmed to suit the layout, review that choice rather than assuming the original image will appear unchanged.

Confirm the intended print location

A front design and a back design are separate instructions. Make sure the proof and order details reflect the correct locations, including any sleeve artwork or smaller chest print. If you are unsure which view you are looking at, ask before approving it.

Discuss the intended print dimensions when size matters. An image displayed on a phone does not give you a physical measurement by itself. A simple mockup helps communicate the idea, while written placement and sizing details help explain what you expect on the finished item.

Review the artwork with the product in mind

Consider the garment color alongside the design. Light and dark details need to be reviewed in context, especially if the same artwork is being used on more than one shirt color. Make sure any alternate versions are included in the discussion rather than assuming one layout covers every product.

For names and numbers, check each personalized variation against your list. For a photo gift, review the selected photo and any accompanying message. The general process is similar, but the details deserving the closest attention depend on what you are ordering.

Send corrections as a clear list

If something needs changing, describe the location and the exact correction. “Change the back phone number to…” gives a clearer instruction than “fix the number.” For wording changes, provide the complete replacement text so there is less room for interpretation.

After corrections are made, review the updated version again. Check the changed area and the rest of the design together before approving. Keep your final artwork approval connected to the agreed garment choices, quantities and deadline so the order details stay consistent.

A careful proof review is a small part of ordering custom printing, but it deserves your attention. Take your time, ask about anything unclear and make sure the design communicates what you want.
