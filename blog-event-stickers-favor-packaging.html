Planning Custom Stickers for Event Favors and Packaging

A custom sticker can carry a thank-you message, identify a party favor or help a small business make its packaging feel familiar. When you’re planning one for an event, start with what happens after somebody receives it. Will they read it on a bag, keep it as a souvenir or use it to tell similar packages apart? That answer helps you choose the words and layout.

At Joe’s Unique Creations in Glenside, Pennsylvania, I print custom stickers and labels for businesses, schools, organizations and personal projects. Here are a few ways to plan event stickers around the job you want them to do.

Give each sticker a clear purpose

Write down the main thing you want the person to notice. For a family celebration, that could be the occasion and a short thank-you. For a school event, it might be the club name or the year. A business giveaway might put the logo first, with a simple way to recognize the company later.

Decide which details belong on the sticker and which can stay on the invitation, card or event program. You can have several useful pieces of information without squeezing them all into one small design. Try writing the message as plain text first. Once the words feel right, it’s easier to decide how much decoration the layout needs.

Plan around the package people will actually receive

Put the bag, box or container in front of you before choosing a shape. Think about where the sticker will sit when the package is filled and handed over. If it crosses a fold or wraps around a corner, check which part of the message stays visible from the front.

A paper cutout can help you compare two possible layouts. Add a rough sketch of the main words and hold it against the package. My guide to choosing sticker and label sizes goes into the measuring side of that decision. Keep your sample package nearby when reviewing the design so you can picture how the pieces work together.

Use matching versions when the details change

Sometimes an event needs a few related labels. You might want the same design with different table names, group names or messages for different recipients. Decide which parts should match and list the changing text separately. That gives each version a clear identity without starting the design conversation over every time.

Give each version a short label in your order notes and use that label on its artwork file. If you need different quantities, put the quantity beside the version name. Check the total against the number of packages you plan to prepare, including any extras you choose to keep for your own event planning.

Describe how and where the stickers will be used

Tell me what the stickers will go on and what conditions you expect them to face. A paper favor bag used indoors raises different questions from a container you plan to keep in a cooler. Those details help us discuss material options for the project before you settle on the order.

If you want guests to keep the sticker and apply it somewhere else, mention that too. The custom sticker and label page covers the types of projects I take on. Ask about your intended use when you inquire so we can review the options that fit it.

Build time into your packing plan

Give the date you need the printed stickers in hand, along with the event date. If you’re assembling gift bags over several evenings, you may need them earlier than someone who is labeling a few boxes on the morning of a gathering. Confirm availability and timing for your particular order before arranging a packing session. Before approval, check the event name, dates, spelling and quantity of each version. A clear plan makes that final review easier to work through.
