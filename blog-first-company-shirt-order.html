A Practical Plan for Your First Company Shirt Order

Ordering company shirts can feel like a small task until you start collecting sizes, comparing colors and deciding what belongs on the back. A little organization at the beginning can make the whole process easier. You do not need a large company or a finished uniform program to get started.

Here at Joe’s Unique Creations in Glenside, Pennsylvania, I offer business apparel for local crews, shops, service companies and other organizations. If this is your first order, here is a practical way to think through the details before sending your information.

Decide what the shirts need to do

Start with the situation in which people will wear them. Are they everyday employee shirts, clothing for a community event or something your team will wear when meeting customers? The answer helps you choose a layout and garment that suit the purpose instead of picking a design only because it looks good on a screen.

Write down your main goal in one sentence. You might want customers to recognize your crew, make your business name easier to spot or give event volunteers a shared appearance. That goal becomes a useful guide whenever you need to choose between competing design ideas.

Choose the information that matters most

Your business name or logo usually deserves a clear place in the layout. After that, decide whether a phone number, website or short description of your work would help. Try to avoid treating the shirt like a brochure. People need to understand the main message without studying every line.

A left chest logo with a larger back design is one option described on my business apparel page. Other placements may work better for your garment and artwork. Bring your preferences, and we can review what makes sense for the particular order.

Make one reliable size list

Collect each person’s requested size and the quantity needed. If some employees need more shirts than others, record that clearly. Keep colors, styles and any individual names on the same list so you are not trying to combine several different messages later.

Before final approval, add up the quantities by size and compare that total with the number of garments being ordered. This simple check can catch a missing employee or a duplicated line. Ask about the sizing information for the selected garment if someone is unsure about fit.

Send the best logo file available

Look for the original artwork from your designer or your existing business files. Send that version when possible, along with a description of any changes you want. If you only have a photograph or screenshot of your logo, explain that at the beginning so the artwork can be reviewed.

Keep spelling and contact details separate from assumptions about the logo. A phone number in an old image may no longer be the one you want printed. Provide the exact wording you approve, and identify any part of the design that must remain unchanged.

Think ahead to replacement shirts

Your first order may not be your last. Employees join, shirts get replaced and businesses add different garments for different seasons. Keep a copy of the approved design, garment details and placement notes with your business records. Those details provide a helpful reference when you return.

Matching a previous order also depends on garment availability, so mention when consistency is especially important. If you want company hoodies later, discuss how the same identity could carry across different clothing without assuming every layout will transfer unchanged.

Give a real deadline and review the total

Tell me when you need the finished shirts in hand. If they are for an event, include time for sorting and distribution. Share your budget and preferred garment choices early so the conversation stays focused on an order that fits your needs.

Use the current pricing page as a starting point, then confirm your exact combination of garments, sizes and printing. Review names, numbers, quantities and the final design before production. A clear list and one person collecting the final instructions can save a lot of back-and-forth.

A useful company shirt begins with a clear purpose. Once the message, size list and deadline are organized, you have the foundation for an order that represents your business in a consistent way.
