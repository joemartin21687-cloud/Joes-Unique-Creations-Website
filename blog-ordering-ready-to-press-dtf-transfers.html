Ordering DTF Transfers When You Have Your Own Heat Press

Having your own heat press gives you a way to finish custom apparel yourself, but you still need the artwork prepared and the transfers printed. Ready-to-press DTF transfers let you order the printed designs separately from the garments. That can be useful when you already handle the final application and want help with the printing side of a project.

At Joe’s Unique Creations in Glenside, Pennsylvania, I offer custom DTF transfers as well as finished printed apparel. If you are ordering transfers for the first time, the most helpful starting point is a clear description of what you want to receive. Here are the details worth gathering before you send the order.

Make clear that you need transfers only

Start by saying whether you want finished shirts or the transfers themselves. Those are different orders. With ready-to-press transfers, the shop supplies the printed transfer and you handle application to the garment with your own heat press.

If you want me to supply and press the apparel too, say that instead. You can compare the ready-to-press transfer service with the DTF apparel printing service before choosing the route that fits your project. Being clear about that first decision helps keep the rest of the instructions focused.

List each design separately

If your order contains several logos or graphics, give each one a simple name. Use those names consistently when describing sizes and quantities. A list that says “front logo” and “back artwork” is easier to follow than a message referring to “that one” when several files are attached.

Keep variations separate too. If the same design needs different names, numbers or wording, identify each version and how many you need. Review the wording before submitting the files so the printed order reflects the version you intend to use.

Provide the finished dimensions

Tell me the intended width and height of the printed design if you know them. If you are unsure, explain the garment placement and the sizes of garments involved. A smaller chest design and a larger back design should not be left to the same vague instruction.

When discussing measurements, make clear whether you mean the artwork itself or the surrounding sheet. Include the units so inches and another measurement are not confused. If maintaining the original proportions matters, mention that rather than requesting a width and height that may change the shape.

Connect quantities to the correct size

One artwork file may be needed at several sizes. Write the quantity beside each specific design and dimension. That gives us a clear list to review and makes it easier to spot a missing size before printing begins.

Count designs by their intended placement as well. A shirt with a front logo and a back graphic uses two separate printed pieces, so a garment count alone does not fully describe the transfer order. Include any extra transfers you want in your requested quantity rather than assuming they are included.

Send the best artwork you have

The transfer order needs the same attention to artwork as a finished apparel order. Send the original file when possible and explain whether any background is meant to print. My artwork guide covers common file questions and what information should accompany a design.

If the file needs cleanup or you only have an older image, send it for review before assuming it is ready. Describe the result you want and identify details that must remain unchanged. A clear discussion before printing is more useful than discovering a misunderstanding when you are ready to press.

Confirm the application information and timing

Tell me what garments you intend to use and ask for the application instructions for the supplied transfers. Review those instructions with your own equipment and garment information before starting. Avoid treating settings from a different transfer order as automatic instructions for the new one.

Include the date you need the transfers in hand, not just the event date. Leave room in your plan for your own application work and organizing the finished garments. Turnaround needs to be confirmed for the actual artwork, quantity and current production capacity.

A well-organized transfer order brings four things together: the design, its finished dimensions, the quantity and the deadline. Once those details are clear, we can review your project and work out the next step without guessing what should be printed.
