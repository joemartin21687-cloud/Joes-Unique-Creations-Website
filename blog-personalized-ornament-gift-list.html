Planning Personalized Ornaments for Your Holiday Gift List

A personalized ornament has a small space to tell a story. You might want a family name, a child’s drawing, a favorite photograph or a few words that bring somebody to mind. Before putting everything into one design, think about the moment you want the person opening it to recognize. That gives the rest of the layout a direction.

At Joe’s Unique Creations in Glenside, Pennsylvania, personalized ornaments are part of the custom gifts I offer. If you’re getting your holiday list together, here are a few ways to organize your ideas and make the details easier to review.

Choose one main idea for each ornament

Start by finishing this sentence for yourself. “When they see this, I want them to remember…” Your answer might be a family gathering, a pet, a new home or a person they love. You don’t have to explain that whole story in the printed wording. A name and a year might be enough when the photograph already carries the meaning.

If you’re using a child’s drawing, decide which part matters most. Sometimes the handwritten name is as special as the picture. Keep those pieces together when you send the idea, and point out anything you want preserved. If you’re considering adding a typed message, write it separately so you can compare how the ornament feels with and without it.

Look at the shape before settling on the picture

Different ornament shapes leave different spaces for the design. A picture that fits comfortably inside a circle may need a different crop for a tree shape. Look at where the faces, names and important details will sit. Leave some breathing room around them instead of pushing everything right up to an edge.

Try drawing the approximate outline on paper and placing a copy of your picture inside it. This can help you spot a face that gets crowded or a long family name that needs another line. My personalized Christmas ornament page is a starting point for exploring the options. Ask about the particular shape and material you have in mind before building your whole design around it.

Give the wording its own careful check

Write the exact spelling you want printed, including capital letters, punctuation and the year if you want one. Family nicknames can have several spellings, and the version that looks unusual to somebody else may be exactly right for your family. A separate written note helps make that intention clear.

Read the wording aloud, then ask somebody familiar with the name to check it. For a remembrance ornament, give yourself time to choose the message. A simple phrase you would naturally say can feel more personal than a long verse that leaves little room for the picture. You can bring a few possibilities to the conversation before deciding.

Keep a gift list that matches your files

If you’re ordering several ornaments, give each design a short label such as “Grandmom photo” or “New home.” Put that same label beside its quantity, wording and image filename. Two ornaments can use the same photograph while needing different names, so record each version on its own line.

Keep the original photo available when possible. If you only have one copy with text already added, mention that when discussing the design. The artwork file guide explains what to send for review. Include the date you need to give out the gifts, allowing yourself time to wrap and sort them. Confirm availability and timing for your specific order before making arrangements around a pickup date. Once the details are together, you have a useful plan for a gift that feels personal from the moment somebody opens it.
