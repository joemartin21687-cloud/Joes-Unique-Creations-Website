One Custom T-Shirt, Three Ways to Make It Count

Sometimes one shirt is exactly what you need. It might be a birthday gift for someone who’s hard to shop for, a replacement for a crew shirt that’s worn out, or the first chance to see your own artwork on fabric. I take single-shirt orders at Joe’s Unique Creations in Glenside, Pennsylvania. The most useful thing you can tell me is what that one shirt needs to do.

Make a gift that feels like the person wearing it

Think of a detail they’ll recognize right away. It could be a line from a family joke, a drawing from their child, or a photo of a pet they talk about all the time. If the image needs an explanation, a few words can help. If the picture tells the story by itself, give it enough room to be the focus.

Pick a shirt color they actually wear. If you’re between two colors, picture the artwork on each one. A design with dark lettering may be easier to read on a light shirt. If you want a dark shirt because it’s their favorite, tell me which parts of the design need to stand out so we can review the combination together.

For a gift with a name or date, send the spelling exactly as it should appear. Tell me when you need it in hand, especially if you’re giving it away before the event itself. A birthday party on Saturday and a gift you’re packing on Thursday are two different deadlines.

Replace a shirt you already wear

When someone needs one more work shirt, a photo of the old shirt can help show where the print belongs. Take a picture from straight in front and include the back if it was printed. Send the original logo file separately if you have it. The old shirt is a reference for the layout, and the logo file gives us the artwork to review.

Check the label for the size and any style details you can still read. Let me know what matters most if the exact garment is hard to match. You might care more about the fit than the shade, or the shirt might need to look as close as possible to what everyone else on the crew wears. Tell me that up front so we can discuss the right options.

Try out a design before ordering a group run

A single shirt can help you decide how a design should look on the finished garment. If you’re planning shirts for a club or an event, use that first piece to answer a specific question. Can you read the event name from across a room? Does the graphic sit where you expected when someone puts the shirt on? Is there too much detail for the smaller print you had in mind?

Pick a sample size and color that tell you something about the rest of the order. If the group will wear several colors or a wide range of sizes, mention that before settling on a layout. Keep a note of the approved artwork, garment and print location so there’s a clear starting point if you come back for more.

Give me enough to picture the finished shirt

Send the best artwork you have, the shirt size and color, the print location and your deadline. If you’re still choosing between a large front print and a smaller chest design, say what you’re leaning toward and why. Even a simple note like “I want the name to be readable in a group photo” helps guide the layout.
