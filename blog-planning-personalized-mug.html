Planning a Personalized Mug Around the Person Who Will Use It

A personalized mug can have a lot to say without being crowded. Maybe it’s a name that makes somebody smile, a photograph they love or a short message they’ll see during their morning coffee. Before putting those pieces together, think about how the person will hold the mug and what you want them to notice first.

At Joe’s Unique Creations in Glenside, Pennsylvania, custom mugs are part of the personalized drinkware and gifts I offer. Here are a few practical choices to work through when you’re planning a mug for one person or a coordinated set for several people.

Think about which side faces the person

Pick up a mug you already use and hold it the way you normally would. Notice where the handle sits and which part of the mug you can see. Now picture a name, photograph or message in that spot. If you’re giving the mug as a gift, think about whether the main picture is meant for the person holding it to enjoy or for somebody across the table to see.

You can explain your preference with a simple sketch. Draw a circle for the top of the mug, add the handle and mark where you want the main design. That gives us something clearer to discuss than “put it on the front,” which can mean different things depending on how someone holds the mug.

Choose one part to lead the design

If the mug includes a photograph, let that be the starting point. Decide whether the name belongs underneath it, beside it or on another part of the layout. If the message is the main feature, give the words enough space and choose a picture or decoration that supports them.

Write the message out before deciding how many lines it needs. Read it aloud and see whether every sentence belongs on the mug. A few familiar words can feel personal. If you have a longer note you’d like to include with the gift, you can keep that on a separate card and give the mug a shorter message that’s easy to read.

Plan the photograph around the available space

Choose a photo where the important people or details have room around them. When you’re considering the layout, check how close faces are to the edges and whether a crop would remove somebody’s shoulder or part of a meaningful setting. Keep the original image available so we can review the possibilities.

A group photograph and a close portrait may need different layouts. You can bring more than one option if you’re unsure which will fit the idea best. My guide to choosing a photo for a personalized gift offers more ways to narrow down the choice before discussing the artwork.

Give each mug in a set its own clear details

If you’re ordering gifts for several people, decide which parts should match and which parts should change. You might want the same general design with a different name on each mug. Put each name on its own line in your order notes, and connect it to the right photograph or wording.

Use simple labels for your files and list, such as “Aunt Rose” and “Uncle Mark.” These are examples of labels you can use to organize the order. Check spelling with someone who knows the recipient, especially if the gift uses a nickname or an unusual capitalization.

Review the whole layout before approval

Look at the picture, wording and placement together. Check whether the parts you care about have enough room and whether the design still feels balanced around the handle.
