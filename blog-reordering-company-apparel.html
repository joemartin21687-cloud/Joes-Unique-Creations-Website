How to Reorder Company Shirts and Keep Your Crew Consistent

A new employee starts next week, somebody needs a replacement shirt, or your crew is adding hoodies for cooler mornings. Reordering company apparel sounds simple until you’re trying to remember which shirt you picked and whether the logo went on the front, back or both. A few saved details from the first order make that conversation easier.

Here at Joe’s Unique Creations in Glenside, Pennsylvania, I print business shirts and hoodies for local companies and crews. If you want new pieces to look consistent with apparel you already have, here’s what to keep together and what to check before placing the next order.

Save the garment details while you have them

Keep the brand, style name or number, color and size list from your original order. A description like “the navy work shirts” leaves room for questions. A photo of the garment label can help identify what you have, especially when several shirts look similar in a picture. Save that photo in the same folder as your order information.

Write down what your crew liked about the fit, too. If someone wants a different size this time, record their current choice instead of copying the previous list automatically. For a new employee, ask about sizing for the specific garment being considered. The old order is a useful starting point, and the new wearer should still have a chance to make their selection.

Keep the approved logo and layout together

Save the artwork you approved, along with a picture or proof showing the print locations. Label the files so it’s clear which version was used. A business may have a logo for social media, another for signs and an older design sitting in an email attachment. Knowing which one belongs on the shirts helps keep the order consistent.

If you have the original design file, keep it available alongside the proof. A picture of a finished shirt can show placement, but the original artwork gives us a better starting point for the printed design. My artwork file guide explains the kinds of files you can send for review. Mention any details that need to match, including the lettering, colors and size of the design.

Make changes easy to spot

Before sending your reorder, separate the details you want repeated from the details you want changed. You might want the same chest logo but a new phone number on the back. Put that change in writing and check every digit. If the company name or website has changed, review all the places it appears in the artwork.

When adding hoodies or another garment style, ask to review the layout for that item. A shirt proof can help explain your idea, while a hoodie’s hood and pocket deserve their own placement check. My hoodie placement guide has a few practical ways to picture the design before deciding.

Check availability before promising an exact match

Garment availability can change between orders. Tell me early if matching your previous shirts is important, and include the original order details when you inquire. If the exact garment is unavailable, discuss the options before choosing a replacement. You may want to compare the fit, color and appearance with something your crew already wears.

Send a fresh quantity by size and the date you need the order in hand. Include time for handing everything out if you’re ordering for an event or a new team member. The company apparel page covers the information that helps me review a business order. Once the order is approved, save the updated details with the previous version. That little record gives the next person organizing shirts something clear to work from.
