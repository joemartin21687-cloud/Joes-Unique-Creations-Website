Planning Shirts for School Event Volunteers and Coaches

When you’re organizing shirts for a school event, it’s easy to picture the students or players first. Then you remember the coaches, the people checking everyone in and the volunteers helping families find their way. A useful shirt plan takes those jobs into account before the artwork is finished.

At Joe’s Unique Creations in Glenside, Pennsylvania, I offer custom apparel for schools, teams, clubs and event groups. If you’re helping organize a local event, here’s how to think through what the adults and volunteers need from their shirts while keeping the group’s overall look together.

Start with the jobs people will be doing

Write down the roles that need to be easy to recognize. You might have coaches, registration helpers, activity leaders and people directing visitors. Decide whether everyone can wear the same design or whether a few roles need their own wording. Keep that wording short enough for somebody to read while walking toward the wearer.

Think about where people will stand. A helper behind a table may need a clear front design because visitors won’t see their back. A volunteer leading a group through a building might benefit from wording people can recognize from behind. Sketch both views before choosing which information belongs on each side.

Choose the message people should notice first

A school logo can connect everyone to the event, while a word like “Volunteer” can help a visitor know whom to approach. Decide which needs to stand out most on each version. Give that part room instead of trying to make the event name, school name, date and role equally large.

Print a rough version of the wording on paper and look at it from a few steps away. Ask someone who hasn’t been working on the design what they notice first. Their answer can show whether the layout is doing what you intended. If the role disappears among decorative details, try giving it a separate line or simplifying the surrounding words.

Agree on colors and garment choices together

If you want different colors for different roles, write down exactly what each color means. Keep that choice consistent in the order list and in the instructions you give your helpers. Color can support the wording, and a clearly printed role gives visitors another way to identify the person they need.

Ask about garment options for the actual event. Someone helping outdoors may have different preferences from someone working at an indoor table. Include youth and adult sizes where needed, and confirm the selections with the people wearing them. The school and team apparel page explains the kinds of group orders we can discuss.

Keep each version easy to identify

Give every design a simple label, such as “Coach front” or “Volunteer back.” Use those same labels beside the quantities and garment choices. If two versions share the same logo but have different words underneath, make that difference clear in your written instructions.

Have one organizer gather corrections and send the final details together. You can use my guide to organizing a school or team apparel roster to connect sizes with the right personalization. Check the spelling of roles and the event name along with the quantities. Small wording differences can be easy to miss when you’re looking at several similar designs.

Plan the handout before the event begins

Include the date you need to distribute the shirts when discussing timing. You may want helpers to collect them before an early setup shift or a practice meeting. Confirm availability and the production plan for your particular order before telling the group when everything will be ready. Once the shirts are handed out, make sure everyone knows the role their design represents and where they’ll be helping. That connects the printing plan with the people doing the work.
