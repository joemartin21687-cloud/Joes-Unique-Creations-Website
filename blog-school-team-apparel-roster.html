How to Build a Clear Roster for School and Team Apparel

A school or team apparel order can involve a surprising number of moving parts. Players want their own names and numbers, coaches may need a different style, and families may want matching fan shirts. The artwork brings everyone together, but the roster is what connects each finished garment to the right person.

At Joe’s Unique Creations in Glenside, Pennsylvania, I offer school shirts, team apparel and spirit wear for local groups. A clear order list helps us discuss the details without trying to piece together instructions from scattered messages. Here is a practical way to build one.

Give every garment its own clear entry

Start with the person receiving the item, the garment type, the requested size and the quantity. When personalization is involved, add the exact printed name and number to that same entry. Keeping those details together makes it easier to see what belongs on each garment.

If someone orders both a shirt and a hoodie, use separate entries. The size or printed wording may be different between the two items. A separate line also gives you a place to record a change without accidentally changing the instructions for everything that person ordered.

Separate youth and adult sizing

A size marked only as “medium” can leave an important question unanswered. Write out whether it is a youth or adult size, along with the selected garment style. Ask about the sizing information for that particular garment before collecting final choices from the group.

My school and team apparel page explains that orders can include mixed sizes when the selected garments are available. Availability should be checked before promising every family the same combination of style, size and color. Make a note of any acceptable alternatives rather than assuming a substitution is fine.

Copy names exactly as they should print

Ask each person to check the spelling they want on the garment. A roster used for registration may show a full legal name, while the requested apparel may use a surname or nickname. Record the approved printed wording rather than expecting the printer to choose between versions.

Pay attention to punctuation, spaces and capitalization. If a name contains an apostrophe or a hyphen, include it clearly. For numbers, record the intended appearance as well. A request for “07” should stay written that way if both digits are meant to appear.

Keep shared artwork separate from individual details

Your group may use one mascot or event design across the order while changing only the back name and number. Describe that shared layout once, then identify any exceptions on the relevant garment entries. This keeps the main instructions readable without hiding special requests.

Send the artwork files with the list and identify which version is approved for review. If sponsor graphics or event wording are still changing, say so. An unfinished sponsor list can affect the layout, so it helps to know what is settled before the design is treated as final.

Include coaches, volunteers and supporters

Think beyond the player list. Coaches, staff, volunteers and family members may want coordinated apparel with different wording. Record those items with the same care as the personalized player garments so they do not become an extra set of instructions added at the last minute.

For example, a coach title and a player surname are different printing instructions even when the garment color is the same. Clearly label which entries follow the shared team layout and which need a separate role or message. You can also review hoodie options if the group wants more than shirts.

Review totals and set a final list

Before submitting, total the garments by style and size. Compare those totals with your individual entries. Then check every personalization against the person who approved it. Having one organizer collect corrections can prevent two conflicting versions from circulating.

Include your needed-by date and allow for distributing the order before the event. Check the current pricing information, and have the final garment choices, printing details and availability confirmed for your order. Keep any later changes clearly marked rather than quietly replacing an earlier list.

A thoughtful roster does not need to be complicated. It needs to be clear enough that another person can understand each item without guessing. Once that list is ready, the conversation can focus on bringing your school or team design together.
