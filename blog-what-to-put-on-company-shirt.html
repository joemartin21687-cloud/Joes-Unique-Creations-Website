What Information Belongs on Your Company Shirt?

A company shirt has a limited amount of space to tell someone who you are. Your logo may be familiar to regular customers, while somebody seeing your crew for the first time might need a little more information. Before adding every service and contact detail, decide what you want a person to remember after a quick look.

At Joe’s Unique Creations in Glenside, Pennsylvania, I print custom business apparel for local companies and crews. Here’s a practical way to choose the wording for a work shirt and give the important information room to stand out.

Make the business name easy to find

Start with the name customers know you by. Look at your logo and ask whether someone unfamiliar with it can read that name without an explanation. A decorative mark can be part of your identity, and the lettering still needs its own space in the layout.

Try a paper version of the design at roughly the size you’re considering. Put it on a shirt and take a few steps back. Ask another person what they read first. If the name gets lost among smaller details, consider making it more prominent or moving some information to another print location.

Choose a short description of what you do

If the name doesn’t explain the business, a short service description can help. Think about the words a customer would use when asking for your work. A clear description gives them something useful to connect with the name.

You don’t have to fit your complete service list into the design. Choose the work you most want the shirt to represent, then review the wording for clarity. If several services belong together, see whether a simple description covers them without a long row of tiny words. Read it aloud and decide whether it sounds like something you would naturally tell a new customer.

Pick the contact detail people should use

Decide which contact method makes sense for the shirt. You might want people to call a business number, remember a website or recognize a social account. Choose the detail you expect to keep using and make sure it’s written consistently with your other business materials.

Check every digit and letter against the original contact information. Then ask somebody else to check it independently. A familiar number can be surprisingly easy to read past when you’ve looked at the same design several times. Keep the confirmed wording in a separate note so it’s clear which version is approved.

Give the front and back different jobs

A small front logo can identify the wearer during a conversation. A larger back layout can give the name, service description or contact information more room. Think about how your crew meets customers and which side people are likely to see.

If you’re planning shirts for people who spend time behind a counter or table, review the front view carefully. If the back carries the main message, look at the whole arrangement together instead of judging each line on its own. My business apparel page covers common print placements and the details needed to discuss a company order.

Check the wording on every garment version

If your order includes shirts and hoodies, review both layouts. Decide where the shared information belongs on each garment and make sure the contact details match. The guide to mixed shirt and hoodie orders can help you organize those versions.

Before approval, compare the design with your final written notes. Check the business name, service wording, contact information and any employee personalization. Keep a copy of the approved artwork for future additions or replacements. Include your garment choices, quantities by size and needed-by date when discussing the order, and confirm availability and timing for that specific combination. Clear wording gives the finished apparel a purpose you can explain in a sentence.
