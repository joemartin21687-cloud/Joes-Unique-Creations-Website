WHAT IS DTF PRINTING, EXACTLY?

How DTF works in plain terms.

DTF stands for Direct-to-Film. Here's the simple version of how it works:

  1. Your artwork is printed onto a special transfer film using full-color inkjet printing.
  2. Adhesive powder is applied to the wet ink and then cured with heat.
  3. The finished transfer is heat pressed onto your garment.
  4. Result: a vibrant, durable, full-color print that bonds to the fabric.

Because there are no color separations or screens, every color in your design prints exactly as intended — including gradients, shading, fine lines, and photographic images. That's why DTF is such a strong fit for complex logos and custom artwork.

COMMON QUESTIONS

DTF vs screen printing FAQ

What is DTF printing?

DTF (Direct-to-Film) printing prints your artwork onto transfer film, applies adhesive, and heat presses it onto the garment. It produces full-color prints with no color minimum and no setup fees.

What is screen printing?

Screen printing uses a separate physical screen (stencil) for each color in a design. It's cost-effective at high quantities but has per-color setup fees that make small orders expensive.

Is DTF or screen printing better for small orders?

DTF is better for small orders. There are no setup costs, so even a single shirt is affordable. Screen printing becomes more cost-effective for very large, simple-design runs.

Does DTF printing last as long as screen printing?

DTF prints are very durable with proper care (wash inside-out, cool water, low heat). For high-volume workwear washed frequently, screen printing may have a slight edge in extreme durability, but for most personal and business use DTF holds up well.
