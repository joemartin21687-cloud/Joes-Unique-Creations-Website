What is DTF printing?

DTF stands for Direct-to-Film. The design is printed in full color onto transfer film, coated with adhesive powder, cured, and then heat pressed onto the garment. It works well for detailed logos, photos, gradients, and colorful artwork.

Why do you use DTF printing?

DTF is our primary apparel-printing method because it produces bright, detailed, durable full-color graphics and works on many common garment fabrics and colors.

What fabrics can DTF be printed on?

DTF can work on many cotton, polyester, and blended garments. The exact garment and fabric should be checked before production because heat tolerance and surface treatments can vary.

Can you print full-color photos on shirts?

Yes. DTF is well suited for full-color artwork, photographs, gradients, and detailed designs when the source image is high enough quality.

Can you print white ink on dark shirts?

Yes. DTF printing uses a white underbase so full-color designs can be applied to dark garments, including black shirts and hoodies.

What file types should I send for artwork?

High-resolution PNG files with transparent backgrounds are ideal for many DTF jobs. Vector files such as SVG, EPS, AI, or PDF can also be useful. High-resolution JPG files may work when transparency is not needed.

How high resolution should my artwork be?

For raster artwork, a clean high-resolution image at the intended print size is best. Small screenshots, social-media thumbnails, and heavily compressed images may print poorly or require cleanup.

How should I wash a DTF printed shirt?

For longer print life, turn the garment inside out, wash in cool or cold water with mild detergent, avoid bleach, and use low heat or hang dry when possible. Avoid ironing directly over the printed design.

How durable are DTF prints?

A properly produced and applied DTF print is designed to be durable for normal wear and washing. Garment care, wash temperature, dryers, and direct heat can affect long-term appearance.

Can I iron directly over a DTF design?

No. Avoid placing a household iron directly on the printed design. If ironing is necessary, turn the garment inside out and avoid direct high heat on the transfer.
